Research · Miami University CRAFT Lab · 2025–present

Sheet metal forming research

I'm an undergraduate author and researcher in the CRAFT Lab, working alongside graduate students on two papers. The walkthrough on this page shows one of the methods: estimating hardness anywhere on a formed sheet from a set of test points.

Review paperUndergraduate author

A comprehensive review of the major sheet metal forming methods and how digital twins, robotics and machine learning are being integrated into them.

Technical paperUndergraduate author

A physics-informed machine learning (PIML) architecture that predicts the geometric outcome of sheet metal specimens formed with the English wheel process.

How the interpolation works

  1. Hardness is measured at 87 test points across a 12.25 × 9.75 in sheet.
  2. The points are joined into a Delaunay triangulation.
  3. For any spot on the sheet, the walkthrough finds the triangle that contains it and computes barycentric weights for its three corners.
  4. The weights blend the three corner readings into a predicted hardness. Outside the tested area it falls back to inverse-distance weighting.

Industry work · Volynt Aero and MAC-Piranha Aero

Alloy Composition Matcher

I built this materials tool for my work with Volynt Aero and MAC-Piranha Aero. Enter a measured chemistry and it identifies the alloy, ranks US-available alternatives and exports a report. Volynt Aero is developing Vyper, an unmanned hybrid-electric eVTOL cargo aircraft, in Oxford, Ohio.

How it works

  1. Enter the measured compositionElement symbols and wt%, with the last element optionally set as the balance.
  2. Check every gradeEach specified element must fall inside the grade's min/max. Unspecified elements are held to residual or "others, each" limits.
  3. Rank the matchesBy base metal, then out-of-range elements, unmeasured required elements, total deviation and specificity.
  4. Find US-available alternativesA 0–100% equivalence score weighs tensile strength, yield, hardness, elongation, hardenability (ASTM A255 ideal critical diameter) and optional cost.
  5. Export the reportPDF or PNG with the composition, element-by-element checklists, alternatives, method and references.

Experimental Aircraft Club · 2025–present

Ultra-light single-seat aircraft

Founder · Design and fabrication lead

I founded Miami University's Experimental Aircraft Club so undergraduates can take a real, safety-critical aircraft through the whole engineering lifecycle: concept, structural analysis, material selection, fabrication, testing and flight. The design is a single-occupant ultra-light developed with Mini-MAX USA, aligned with FAR Part 103 performance constraints and low wing-loading requirements.

  1. 1

    Mid-wing, rib and spar

    Exposed wooden ribs and spars, built for fabric covering and efficient low-Reynolds-number flight. Metal brackets at the wing–fuselage joint keep it torsionally rigid and carry loads safely.

  2. 2

    Single-occupant cockpit bay

    A simplified cockpit bay sits ahead of the fuselage truss.

  3. 3

    Trussed wooden structure

    The fuselage and wing skeleton are almost entirely lightweight wood, with aluminum brackets and sheet-metal fittings at the high-load joints. A tapered truss boom runs back to the tail.

  4. 4

    Open-frame stabilizers

    Vertical and horizontal stabilizers are open frames, kept light and simple to cover in fabric.
